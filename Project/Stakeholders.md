# Stakeholder Analysis

| Stakeholder | Role | Interest / Expectations | Influence on the System | How the System Affects the Stakeholder |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Students and staff who have lost an item | Primary end users | Recover belongings quickly with minimal effort, and be informed as soon as a possible match exists | Medium. Their reporting behaviour and the quality of the descriptions they write directly determine how well matching performs | Replaces an informal search across group chats and notice boards with a single point of contact, but requires them to trust the system with an AUS ID and contact details |
| Students and staff who have found an item | Primary end users | A quick, low-effort way to hand in an item without needing to locate the owner themselves | Medium. If reporting is inconvenient, found items are never entered and the system has nothing to match against | Provides a clear alternative to leaving an item where it was found or keeping it |
| Campus security and administrative staff | System operators and claim verifiers | Manage reports efficiently, prevent items being released to the wrong person, and resolve disputed claims | High. They approve every release, so their judgement is part of the system's core process, and their workflow requirements shape the admin dashboard | Reduces paper records and walk-in enquiries, but adds a responsibility to review and approve claims through the dashboard |
| University IT department | Technical support and hosting | A secure, maintainable system that meets university standards for handling student data | High. Their requirements constrain technology choices, hosting, and how authentication and personal data are handled | Adds a further system to support, monitor, and secure over the long term |
| University administration | Project sponsor | A professional system that reflects well on the university and reduces lost-property complaints | Medium. Controls whether the system is adopted beyond the course and whether resources are allocated to it | May reduce complaints and liability relating to unclaimed property if the system is adopted more widely |
| Course instructor and lab instructor | Project supervisors and assessors | Correct application of the software engineering process, with decisions that are justified rather than merely listed | High. Feedback on each lab directly shapes the direction of the project | Assesses the team's artifacts and the project outcome |
